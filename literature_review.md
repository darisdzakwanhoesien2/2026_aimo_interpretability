## 0. The competition's own paper

**Štefánik, Mondorf, Waldis, Q. Liu, C. Yang, Spiegel, Kuchař, Kadlčík,
Vawda-Oomerjee, C. Liu, Frieder, Plank, Barez, Stenetorp — "AIMO Interpretability
Challenge"**, [arXiv:2607.13899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13899).

Abstract's framing: *"strong final-answer accuracy does not reveal whether a
model relies on stable reasoning mechanisms or exploits brittle reasoning
shortcuts."* Resources provided: (1) newly-published olympiad problems + symbolic
representations for generating **functional variants**, (2) access to frontier
reasoning models, (3) precomputed **adversarial-robustness assessments** on these
problems. Deliverable: an open robustness benchmark + baseline systems.
(Full PDF unread — still need the formal robust/spurious definition and the
baseline numbers from the paper itself; the operational definition below is
reverse-engineered from the released data.)

## 1. How the label is actually defined (from `examples/baselines/data/`)

The public baseline data (`math-robust-final.csv`, 675 rows; `math-robust-agg.csv`,
141 problems) is **only `qwen3-8b:low`** on Hendrycks MATH problems. Columns:
`base_accuracy`, `permuted_accuracy`, `absolute_accuracy_decay`,
`relative_accuracy_decay`, `n_detrimental_permutations`, `permutations_causing_decay`,
`model_is_robust`.

Empirically, on the 141 aggregated (`OVERALL`) problems:

- `model_is_robust == True`  ⟺  `absolute_accuracy_decay == 0.0` **exactly** (all 36 True rows; `n_detrimental_permutations == 0`).
- `model_is_robust == False` ⟺  `absolute_accuracy_decay ∈ [0.5, 1.0]` (105 rows, mean 0.835). No problem sits between 0 and 0.5.

So **robust = the model solved the base problem and *every* perturbation family
left accuracy unchanged; spurious = at least one family caused a large drop.**
This is a hard threshold on adversarial-robustness experiments the organizers ran
offline — not a mechanistic judgment. Our system is asked to *predict that
experiment's outcome* from the problem + model alone.

**Label balance is heavily skewed: ~75% spurious (False).** A constant `False`
predictor scores ~0.74 accuracy on this data — and accuracy is the leaderboard
metric (§4). The bar is not 0.5.

### Perturbation families (`permutation_type` in the data)

`distract`, `rename`, `rephrase`, `typos`, `domain`. Variants are generated by
`gpt-oss-120b` (`permutation_source = gpt-oss-120b-gguf_low`). **`permutation_type`
is NOT given to submissions at evaluation** (confirmed in `ingestion.py` and both
baseline READMEs — only `id`, `model_id`, `problem` reach the method).

**→ Design decision:** frame this as predicting accuracy-decay-under-perturbation,
same target the official baselines regress (`absolute_accuracy_decay`) then
threshold. Since we don't get `permutation_type`, a strong method must *internally
reason about / simulate* these five perturbation families. And any method must be
evaluated against the ~0.74 majority-class baseline, not 0.5 — see `plan.md` §2.

## 2. Robustness of mathematical reasoning under perturbation — the lineage

- **GSM-Symbolic** (Mirzadeh et al., Apple, [arXiv:2410.05229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05229)) — accuracy varies across symbolic re-instantiations of the same GSM8K item and drops up to ~65% when one irrelevant-looking clause is added. This is essentially the `distract` + `domain` families at sentence scale, and the evidence that final-answer accuracy hides brittleness.
- **GSM-IC** (Shi et al., 2023, [arXiv:2302.00093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00093)) — irrelevant sentences in the problem collapse accuracy; explicit "ignore irrelevant info" instructions and self-consistency both mitigate. Direct precedent for the `distract` family.
- **Putnam-AXIOM** (Gulati et al., ICML 2025) (unread) — frontier models drop sharply on variable-renamed / constant-altered variants of Putnam problems vs. the originals → evidence that some "reasoning" is memorized-solution retrieval. Precedent for `rename`.
- **AIME/olympiad contamination analyses** (unread) — motivates why AIMO 3 problems are *newly published* and unseen.

**→ Design decision:** the five families are not equally hard to detect from the
base problem. `rename`/`typos` sensitivity should correlate with shallow lexical
dependence; `domain`/`distract` sensitivity with poor problem-structure
abstraction. Features should target both. See `plan.md` §5 (self-generated variant
consistency, one probe per family).

## 3. Interpretability signals — what the official baselines use

### 3a. Representation probing (baseline #1)

`examples/baselines`: one forward pass per problem, extract the **final
input-token hidden state from every layer**, then linear-probe (or RBF-kernel
regress, optionally PCA-reduced) each layer for `absolute_accuracy_decay`, with
repeated `StratifiedGroupKFold` CV (5 seeds, 4 folds) grouping identical problems.
Uses the `holmes-evaluation` framework ([Holmes benchmark](https://holmes-benchmark.github.io/),
Waldis et al. — a co-organizer; branch `probe_only`).

Background: **Alain & Bengio 2016** (linear probes as layer readouts);
**Belinkov 2022 survey** ([arXiv:2102.12452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12452)).

The getting-started `trained-probe` solution ships a schema-v2 **probe ensemble**
(`groups` = CV folds, each with per-seed probes at the chosen layer), scores
`mean(w·h + b − threshold)` over all seed/fold probes at `best_layer_index`,
predicts robust iff mean margin ≥ 0. Artifact stores the HF `model_id` +
`system_prompt` used for encoding; **no precomputed hidden states**.

**→ Design decision:** this is our Tier-A / A+ backbone. Reproduce it first
(establishes the harness + a real number). Extend with: multiple token positions
(not just last), multiple layers as features rather than one, and the answer-token
hidden state after a short generation.

### 3b. Uncertainty profiling (baseline #2)

getting-started `uncertainty-profiling`: greedy-decode one response per problem
(≤2048 prompt / ≤4096 new tokens, static KV cache, batch 8), compute **14
aggregate token-distribution features** — `mean_logprob`, `mean_prob`, `mean_nll`,
`ppl`, `min/max/std_logprob`, **Min-K% logprob** (k=0.20), `mean_entropy`,
`mean_top1_prob`, `mean_top2_margin`, `frac_selected_is_top1`,
`frac_high_conf_tokens` (p≥0.9), `frac_low_conf_tokens` (p≤0.1) — then a
scikit-learn regressor (RF / ExtraTrees / HistGBR, selected by grouped OOF
balanced accuracy) predicts `absolute_accuracy_decay`; threshold → bool.

Checked-in artifact (RF, 500 trees): **grouped OOF balanced accuracy 0.6262,
plain accuracy 0.6879, MAE 0.3429, threshold 0.5760.** Note plain accuracy
(0.69) is *below* the ~0.74 majority-class baseline — this method trades raw
accuracy for actually detecting the minority (robust) class.

Explicitly follows **"Tracing Uncertainty in Language Model Reasoning"**
([arXiv:2605.07776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07776)) — treats reasoning tokens
as evolving states and uses the *shape* of their uncertainty to predict
correctness; the baseline uses only aggregate features, not the paper's temporal
slope/linearity trace features. **A method that adds those temporal features is a
sanctioned, cited improvement path.**

Related: **Min-K% Prob** (Shi et al., [arXiv:2310.16789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16789), membership inference — reused here as a confidence feature); **Kadavath et al. 2022** "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know" ([arXiv:2207.05221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221), P(True) self-eval); **semantic entropy**, Kuhn et al. [arXiv:2302.09664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09664) / Farquhar et al. *Nature* 2024.

**→ Design decision:** Tier-A feature set = the 14 baseline features + temporal
trace features (uncertainty slope, linearity, final-third mean, # of spikes) +
self-consistency spread over k samples + P(True). Regress `absolute_accuracy_decay`,
not the bool, then tune the threshold on the leaderboard metric with the real
class prior.

### 3c. Deeper interpretability (writeup track / Small models)

- **SAEs** — Bricken et al. 2023; Cunningham et al. [arXiv:2309.08600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08600); Templeton et al. 2024. Feasible on Olmo-3-7B / DeepSeek-R1-Qwen3-8B within budget. A "shortcut" feature family is a writeup-prize result.
- **Activation patching / causal tracing** — Meng et al. ROME [arXiv:2202.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262); Wang et al. IOI [arXiv:2211.00593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00593). Patch residual stream between a problem and a self-generated functional variant to localize whether the answer is computed from the quantities or a template.
- **Tuned Lens** — Belrose et al. [arXiv:2303.08112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08112): answer-formation trajectory across layers as a robustness signal.
- **Olmo 3** is fully open (weights + data + code) — the natural Small-track subject for any training-data-contamination or circuit analysis.

**→ Design decision:** Tier-C, Small track, writeup only — not on the scored path.

## 4. The metric shapes the method (from `scoring.py`)

- `accuracy = correct / total(all labels)`; `coverage = covered / total`. **Invalid or uncovered predictions count as wrong for accuracy**, and `coverage` must be exactly 1.0.
- `ingestion.py` **batches cases by `model_id`** and calls `are_robust(model_id, problems)` **once per model** with all its problems. Any exception, wrong length, or non-`bool` entry invalidates that model's *entire* batch.
- Ranking tiebreak = mean runtime over 3 trials; dedicated $5,000 writeup prize for efficient methods.

**→ Design decision:** (a) `solution.py` must wrap everything and *never raise* — on any internal error, fall back to `[False]*n` for that batch. (b) Beat 0.74, not 0.50 — a method must add accuracy over "always spurious", i.e. correctly flip true-robust cases without misclassifying too many spurious ones. (c) Keep the expensive path gated so runtime stays low (`plan.md` §6).
